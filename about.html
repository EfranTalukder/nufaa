About

A marketplace that gives
part of the margin back.

Nufaa is a cashback e-commerce platform for The Gambia, registered as a company in the United Kingdom and in The Gambia. Buyers get between 10% and 20% of an order back, and everything is priced, settled and rewarded in Gambian Dalasi. Crypto is accepted as a way to get money in, and nothing more.

Company registration is not the same as financial regulation. Nufaa is not a bank and does not hold a banking or deposit-taking licence in either jurisdiction. The wallet is closed-loop: it exists to pay for goods on the Nufaa marketplace.

Founder

Nufaa was started by a Gambian founder living between London and Banjul, after years of watching money move in one direction and never come back.

The problem

Nobody in Serrekunda needs a lecture about saving.

The weekly shop happens in cash, at whatever the price is that morning, with no record of what was paid. Cashpower is bought in small amounts because a large amount is not affordable in one go. Airtime is bought the same way. None of it leaves a trace, and none of it earns anything.

Meanwhile the money that reaches families from London, Madrid or New York arrives at a bad rate, sits in cash, and loses value between the day it lands and the day it is spent. The problem was never that people were careless with money. The problem was that the system offered them nothing on the way through.

A marketplace fixes a surprising amount of that at once. Prices are visible before you commit. Orders leave a receipt. And because sellers are competing for the order, some of their margin can go back to the buyer instead of disappearing into the spread. That is all cashback is here — not a giveaway, and not a loyalty scheme with points that expire, but a share of a real margin, paid in the currency people actually live in.

The approach

Dalasi first, always

Prices, wallet, cashback and seller settlement are all in Dalasi. Nobody should have to learn a second currency to buy rice.

No interest, in either direction

There is no credit, no lending and no interest paid on balances. The business earns from commission on completed sales.

Crypto as a rail, not a product

Accepting stablecoin means diaspora money arrives faster and cheaper. It is converted immediately — customers never carry the volatility.

Verified on both sides

Buyers and sellers both pass KYC. It slows the first five minutes and removes most of the trouble that follows.

The plan, in order

Registration first.
Then trade. Then crypto.

We are deliberately not launching everything at once. Each step has to work in Dalasi before the next one is added on top.

  1. Done Step 1

    Register in the UK and The Gambia

    Two entities, two sets of obligations, one business. Selling starts with payments taken in Dalasi and cashback paid in Dalasi — the simplest version of the model, proven before anything is layered on it.

  2. In build Step 2

    Put the whole loop in the app

    Top up, browse, buy, list, sell, withdraw. One place, priced in Dalasi from the first screen to the receipt, so a seller and a buyer are using the same tool.

  3. Next Step 3

    Accept crypto at checkout

    Customers pay with USDT, USDC or BTC. Conversion happens before confirmation, the seller still receives Dalasi, and the cashback is still Dalasi.

  4. Planned Step 4

    Ordering from the diaspora

    Pay from abroad for a basket collected in Banjul or Brikama, with the cashback credited to the family member who picks it up rather than to the sender.

Built for the household budget

Nufaa operates a retail marketplace and a closed-loop wallet used to pay for goods bought on that marketplace. It is not a bank, does not take deposits, does not lend, and does not pay interest.
